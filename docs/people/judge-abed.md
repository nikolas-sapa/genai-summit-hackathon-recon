## 0. TL;DR — the judge in one paragraph

Abed is a self-taught Syrian-Greek engineer who went from refugee-services coding bootcamp student (Social Hackers Academy, 2018) to SF hackathon circuit regular (1st @ Wordware×OpenClaw, 2nd @ Cursor×a16z) in ~7 years. He judges hackathons the way he wins them: **a live, working demo of an agent doing real work end-to-end, with visible taste in the details.** He coined "aura points" — and his own timeline tells you exactly what it means: aura = authentic craft + wit + anti-slop. High aura: an agent that genuinely does something, microcopy that shows a human cared, a concept clever enough to make builders grin. Low aura: bots doing fake GTM, pitch-deck polish over working software, corporate arrogance, anything that smells automated, generic, or soulless. His hero is Paul Graham — he prints PG's essays on paper to read them, built a repo distilling PG essays into agent skills, and the hackathon site links straight to paulgraham.com/good.html ("make something people want"). He is not impressed by credentials, funding talk, or market sizing. He is impressed by proof-of-work, playfulness, and precision. **The winning demo for him is Origami-shaped: an ambitious, slightly meta concept that actually runs live, wrapped in small tasteful details that signal a human sweated it.**

## 6. "Aura" — operational definition

In his vocabulary (X-slang "aura" = social capital/coolness; "low aura" = cringe + tryhard + inauthentic), **aura points ≈ the composite of authenticity, craft, and wit that can't be faked:**

1. **Authentic effort** — a human actually made this, this weekend. Bots-as-substitute-for-effort = low aura; agents-as-multiplier-of-craft = high aura. (The paradox resolves: he hates automation that fakes *presence*, loves automation that does *work*.)
2. **Anti-slop** — no template-y AI-generated sameness. One clever detail ("hand it to your clanker", a split-flap sound, a Tekken-style cut) certifies a human was in the loop.
3. **Playful competence** — serious engineering wearing a joke (Origami 🪽, Claweyes, clawlaland). If the demo makes the room laugh *and* works, that's max aura.
4. **Taste the builder chose to show** — restraint + intentionality: calm UI, good type, meaningful motion, thoughtful naming. PG-essay taste, not Dribbble taste.
5. **Shoot-your-shot energy** — his own career is a chain of audacious asks (Replit/PG London, YC hackathons, cold-starting a summit hackathon). Boldness in concept reads as aura.

## 7. What a winning project/demo looks like through his eyes

**Project shape:**
- An agent that **does real work end-to-end** on "the tedious, multi-step jobs nobody wants to do twice" — his literal brief — ideally something a normal person would actually want (PG's "make something people want" is linked on the site).
- Bonus aura for **embodied/meta shapes** — his winners give agents eyes (Claweyes) or a body/app they construct themselves (Origami). Agents that act in the world > agents that report about the world.
- Useful > impressive-sounding. But a genuinely novel mechanism beats a safe CRUD wrapper.

**Demo shape:**
- **LIVE.** "Make it work and show us the demo — this is not a pitching competition, it's an agent at work." He'd rather see a real run with a rough edge than a polished video. Show the agent *thinking and doing* in real time — the multi-step chain, tool calls, the recovered error. That's the "agent at work" he's literally asking for.
- **One moment of delight** engineered in — sound, motion, a microcopy wink. It's his signature move (flapboard's confetti); it will read as "one of us."
- **Minimal preamble.** Under ~20 seconds of context, then the thing running. Names/wordplay land well with him; market-size slides actively hurt.
- **Honesty about what's real** — he demos himself constantly; fake-it tells are obvious to him. If something's stubbed, say so; authenticity is scored, it's the rubric.
- Craft signals under the hood if asked: type-safe, real architecture — Polymath's copy says "we sweat the parts nobody sees."

**What bores him / bleeds points:**
- Slides-first pitches, TAM talk, "ecosystem" buzzwords
- Thin LLM wrappers / generic chatbots / yet-another-RAG-demo
- Pre-built projects (the FAQ explicitly punishes substantially-pre-built work — judges weigh the 36h window)
- Slick-but-hollow polish — "judged on… rather than polish alone" is a warning
- Anything inauthentic: canned video demo, fake metrics, overclaimed novelty
- Safe, committee-flavored ideas with no personality

**What excites him / banks aura:**
- Working end-to-end autonomy on a task people genuinely hate doing
- A clever twist that makes builders grin (embodiment, recursion, agents-managing-agents, consumer-app shapes)
- Micro-craft: one perfect piece of copy, one satisfying sound/motion detail, native-feel UX
- Scrappy execution: solo or small team, YouTube-taught energy, "let me cook" confidence without arrogance
- Humor that's *inside the product*, not pasted on the pitch

## 8. Tactical notes for our submission

- Optimize for a **60-90 second live agent run** with visible tool-use narration; one sentence of setup, then go.
- Build for **one real user job** painfully well, not breadth. "People and agents want" — if another agent could use it too, that's catnip (his Claweyes literally made agents the users).
- Name it like he would: short, playful, visual (🪽-adjacent). Invest 30 min in microcopy — he explicitly reads copy as the anti-slop certificate.
- If there's a physical/sensory/hardware or self-referential angle available, take it — that's his aesthetic home turf.
- Never say "revolutionary," "seamless ecosystem," or "market opportunity" on stage. Say what it does, show it doing it, land one joke.
- He respects builders who kept going after rejection — if anything broke during the hack, that's a *feature* of the story, not a weakness.
