# Deep Dossier: Judge/Mentor VC Trio
## Gen AI Summit Hackathon — Athens, 14–15 Nov 2026 (pitch on GenAI Summit 5 stage, SNFCC, Mon 16 Nov; theme "Agents at Work")

Hackathon context (Luma, verified): ~100 builders selected, hosted by Abed Hamami + 100mentors Team + Panathēnea Team, venue Spata. Brief verbatim: *"We're looking for agents that can take a goal, figure out what needs to happen, use the tools available to them, and deliver real work end-to-end, proactively… The only requirement: make it work and show us the demo. This is not a pitching competition, it's an agent at work."* Winning teams pitch on the summit main stage Monday.

All three are Greek-linked ecosystem operators whose day job is evaluating *founding teams*, not products. None is a pure technologist — impress them with team signal, ambition, and a working end-to-end agent, not architecture talk.

### Selection philosophy — verbatim (her own framework essay "Accelerometry," Medium, Dec 15, 2022)
- **Team first, always:** *"The most important part of our investment selection process is around the founding team… We anticipate that products will change and markets will shift, but a strong team will soldier on and win."*
- **Founder-problem history:** *"We love to work with teams that have history with the problem they are solving. You lived it, breathed it, experienced it — as a customer, a patient, an employee…"* → "molecular understanding of a pain point."
- **Technical cofounder mandatory:** *"at minimum one co-founder on the team, such as a CTO, who can lead the technical charge… the best tech is usually built in-house."*
- **CEO = salesperson:** *"we're looking for teams, and most importantly, CEOs, that have a strong sales skillset… How well you present your company… signals how well you can sell your product to future hires, other VC investors, and your customers."*
- **Cofounder dynamics under observation:** *"we are careful to observe if co-founders communicate to one another with respect, integrity and admiration. Co-founder conflict… can directly contribute to the death of your company."*
- **Coachability:** winners are *"coachable, very receptive to feedback… motivated by learning."* Full-time commitment expected.
- **Market:** *"large, growing markets… enough white space"*; *"why now"* + competitive fluency — *"you know how to win."*
- **Traction:** MVP/beta + *"a waitlist of users, key initial partnerships, or revenue"*; wants *"a strong tech moat and robust IP."*
- Second essay ("Defining Success," May 2023): week-1 goal-setting discipline — growth, product, fundraising, customer discovery, team building goals tracked through program.
- LinkedIn (Jan–Feb 2025): vision over milestone-ladders — *"What fundamental truth about the world do you believe in so deeply that you're willing to spend a decade proving it right?"* Deck advice: *"a strong deck anticipates questions, presents data cleanly."* Soft spot stated publicly for immigrant, underrepresented and female founders; diversity "deeply committed."
- Contact she publishes: sylvia.bouloutas@techstars.com (stale post-exit); X/Twitter **@sylviabouloutas**; ~8.9K LinkedIn followers, posts monthly-ish founder advice.

### How to impress her at the hackathon
1. **Demo the agent working end-to-end** — she now operates at an agent company; a faked flow is instantly legible to her.
2. **Say who felt the pain.** "I lived this problem as X" is literally her first screen.
3. **Show the cofounder handshake**: builder + seller split; let the technical lead explain the hard part, the "CEO-type" narrate market/vision. Be visibly respectful in Q&A — she's *watching interactions*, not just slides.
4. **One traction artifact**: a real waitlist, a named design partner, one user quote, an LOI — even 48-hour-old validation beats market-size slides.
5. **Give a 10-year vision sentence**, not "next milestone." Then take her feedback mid-session and visibly incorporate it — coachability is scored.
6. Greek-diaspora identity is a genuine rapport hook (she says so herself); don't manufacture it.

### How to impress her at the hackathon
1. **Nail the "who does what" slide.** Complementary, non-overlapping roles; a technical lead who has clearly shipped; a named owner for go-to-market. A solo hero or vague "we all do everything" reads as a red flag to the person who runs talent DD.
2. **Show you can attract people.** Note an advisor, a mentor, a friend who joined for the weekend, a first hire in mind. Talent gravity is her KPI.
3. **Mission articulation.** She explicitly weights values/mission alignment — one honest "why this problem" sentence beats a TAM chart.
4. **Demonstrate coachability + candor** (she's a certified coach): answer questions directly, name what you don't know, describe a real disagreement the team resolved this weekend.
5. **Diversity & team health are not garnish** to her — mixed-skill, mixed-background teams and humane pacing ("we slept, we delegated") will register.
6. Bonus hook: ask her a sharp *talent* question ("who would you hire first for this?") — she joined to be used; founders who activate investors on their cap table earn her stated respect.

### How to impress him at the hackathon
1. **Build an agent that does real work.** His comparison set is Runway/Reflection/Resolve/Omilia demos — the highest bar of the three for actual agentic depth (tool use, autonomy, end-to-end completion), not a thin GPT wrapper.
2. **Frame the ambition as a company, not a project.** His screening question is literally "can this 10x?" — articulate what this looks like at scale and why now is the inflection.
3. **Be transparent about weaknesses** — Endeavor's panel culture rewards founders who surface risks unprompted; bluffing fails the "transparency… non-negotiable" criterion.
4. **Impact/user-care framing resonates** (his own founder story is neurodiversity/mission-first): show the human the agent helps, with a concrete scenario.
5. **Greece/diaspora link = his mandate.** Any Greek co-founder, Athens build intent, or Greek-market use case makes you legible to the Endeavor pipeline — say it explicitly.
6. Expect and welcome the hard question; his org trains panelists to "probe." Treat grilling as the service he believes it is.
